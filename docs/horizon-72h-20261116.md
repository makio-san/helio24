# 72-hour forecast horizon on the adopted pipeline (2026-11-16)

## Decision

Development basis: the relabelled table `artifacts/relabelled_development_20261111_r1` and the adopted pipeline
`artifacts/walk_forward_v2_20261112_r1` ([`config/walk-forward-v2.json`](../config/walk-forward-v2.json),
[`config/operating-point-v3.json`](../config/operating-point-v3.json)); the basis changed on 2026-11-12 because the
frozen labels covered under 30% of the mapped 2022–2023 M/X flares.

Reading `W3_no_gain`, by the rule frozen in [`config/horizon-72h-v1.json`](../config/horizon-72h-v1.json) before any
result. Widening the target from "M/X flare in the next 24 hours" to "in the next 72 hours" does not bring the alert
tier to the precision target in more years. `m72`, trained and thresholded on the 72-hour label, has alert precision at
least 0.50 in 2014, 2015, 2022; 2022 is a fallback year, so the count without fallback
is 2 of 6 (2014, 2015). Alert accuracy is above the always-negative accuracy in the same three years and
below it in 2016, 2017, 2023. The expectation stated when this experiment was proposed, that the 72-hour horizon would raise
precision clearly, was not confirmed.

- Training on the 72-hour label does not move the model in a consistent direction. `m24`, the model trained on the
  24-hour label and only thresholded against the 72-hour label, differs from `m72` by at most 0.1059 in alert
  precision (the largest difference is in the 2022 fallback year) and 0.0331 in F1. `m72` precision is above `m24` in
  2014, 2022, 2023 and below it in 2015, 2016, 2017.
- 2022 is the only year with a large precision value, and it is a fallback year: `m72` precision 0.7224 at recall
  0.3422 and 37 of 72 positive runs missed.
- 2023, the year with the largest positive share (0.1047), reaches precision 0.4996, 0.0004 below the target.
  2016 and 2017 reach 0.3310 and 0.3942. The two lowest-precision years are the two lowest positive-share
  years (2016 and 2017).
- Relative to the positive share the 72-hour alarm is less selective than the 24-hour control in 2014, 2015, 2016, 2017, 2022, 2023 (lift of
  `m72` against the 24-hour control: see the tables); higher precision values come largely from the larger positive
  share, which the definition alone raises.
- Best-threshold F1 (oracle, evaluation labels) of `m72` lies between 0.4679 and 0.5601.
  F1 of the alert tier lies between 0.4538 and 0.5573. F1 is nowhere near 1.

Consequence: on this evidence the 72-hour horizon is not a route to the project's precision and accuracy goals, and it
would change what an alarm means. No model is promoted and no alert, threshold or dashboard setting is changed. Nothing
was downloaded. The reserved 2024–2025 test was not accessed.

## Why this was tried

The [relabelled cycle-25 diagnostic](cycle25-diagnostic-relabelled-20261112.md) found that 0.7139 and 0.7561 of the alert
false-positive windows of 2022 and 2023 lie within 72 hours of a positive window of the same HARP. Five families of extra
information and a [zero-latency ceiling](history-latency-ceiling-20261112.md) did not raise alert precision. A wider
horizon removes the day-level timing requirement instead of trying to learn it. The user asked for the 72-hour window to
be tried.

## Method

Labels: the table holds one window per HARP per hour with a 24-hour label. A GOES M/X event inside the 72 hours after
forecast time t lies inside the 24-hour interval of some window starting in [t, t+48 h], and all those intervals lie
inside the 72 hours. The 72-hour label is therefore the OR of the 24-hour targets of the windows with forecast time in
[t, t+48 h]. The label is known only when the window at t+48 h exists and no two consecutive existing windows in
[t, t+48 h] are more than 24 hours apart; this depends on which windows exist, never on their labels. Windows with an
unknown label are removed from every role, so windows near the end of a HARP's observed life are lost whatever their
label. `prediction_end` is forecast time plus 72 hours, so the annual fold purges windows whose horizon reaches the next
role.

Candidates, features, recipes: only the 96 SHARP summaries. `m72` uses the unchanged recipes of
[`config/walk-forward-v1.json`](../config/walk-forward-v1.json) trained and sigmoid-calibrated on the 72-hour label.
`m24` is the same recipes trained and calibrated on the 24-hour label of the same retained windows and scored against the
72-hour label; its thresholds are chosen against the 72-hour label of the prior year. Expanding annual walk-forward,
ensemble weight 0.45 logistic and 0.55 XGBoost, no hyperparameter search, 24 constituent fits. Watch thresholds
(prior-year window recall 0.90) and alert thresholds (prior-year precision 0.50, recall floor 0.10, fallback flag)
follow `config/walk-forward-v2.json`; they were written and hashed before any evaluation label was read. The years are
those the adopted pipeline evaluates; 2018–2021 stay out because the 24-hour table has no evaluable positives or lacks a
class in calibration there.

Reading rule: `W1_goal_met_on_72h` if in all six years `m72` has alert precision at least 0.50, is not a fallback year
and has accuracy above the always-negative accuracy; `W2_partial` if it reaches precision 0.50 in more years than the
24-hour control (3 of 6); otherwise `W3_no_gain`.

Counts are overlapping windows; consecutive hourly windows share most of their 72-hour interval. A positive run is a
contiguous group of positive windows of one HARP.

## Limits

Already inspected years: adaptive development evidence, not an independent test. Counts are overlapping windows and
groupings of them, not physical flares. The 72-hour rule removes windows near the end of each HARP's observed life
(233265 of 658975 windows over the development table), so the retained windows differ from the 24-hour table; `m24` is
scored on the same retained windows for this reason. The retained windows have a larger 24-hour positive share
(0.0298) than the whole table (0.0240). The relabelled labels are an association by NOAA SGAS and JSOC `NOAA_ARS`, not
ground truth. The 72-hour label is rebuilt from the 24-hour labels, not from the event list. A 72-hour alarm says "a
flare is expected within three days", not "tomorrow"; it is a different product and its values are not 24-hour
improvements. Positive shares differ by year from 0.0117 to 0.1047, which moves precision at a fixed
threshold rule between years independently of the model. 2016 and 2017 have few positive runs
(6 and 9).
