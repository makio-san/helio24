# Two-tier operating point: what a precision rule delivers (2026-11-10)

## Decision

Not supported as frozen. The [frozen protocol](../config/two-tier-v1.json) asked whether an alert tier whose
threshold is chosen on the prior calibration year for window precision 0.50 delivers precision 0.50, and with it
accuracy above the always-negative accuracy, in at least four of six years. It does so in 3 of 6 (2014, 2015, 2017).
In 2016 and 2023 the delivered precision is 0.40 and 0.43; in 2022 the calibration year 2017 does not reach 0.50
at any threshold above the recall floor, so the rule falls back to its best calibration precision and delivers
0.17. The watch tier is identical to the operating point of
[`config/operating-point-v2.json`](../config/operating-point-v2.json) in every year. No model, probability,
alert or threshold is changed and the reserved 2024–2025 test was not accessed. The protocol was frozen and
committed before any result.

## Why this review

Ten routes (threshold rules, alarm persistence, recalibration, probability trajectory, issued SRS context,
weighting, model grids, TCN, SGAS flare history, SHARP Br map CNN) left precision at window recall 0.90 in the
0.04–0.21 band and accuracy below the always-negative accuracy in every year. Accuracy above always-negative is
the same statement as precision above 0.50. The evaluation-year oracle reaches 0.50 only at recall 0.48 (2014),
0.48 (2015), 0.33 (2016), 0.80 (2017), 0.15 (2022) and 0.07 (2023), so the open question was what a deployable
prior-year rule delivers.

## Method

Frozen baseline (96-summary ensemble, stored stage-one models, zero fits). Watch tier:
`helio24.calibration.recall_threshold` on the prior calibration year at window recall 0.90, required to reproduce
the stored operating-point-v2 threshold. Alert tier: `helio24.two_tier.precision_threshold` on the prior
calibration year; among candidate thresholds with calibration recall at least 0.10, the one with the highest
recall whose calibration precision reaches the target (0.30 and 0.50; 0.50 primary); if none reaches it, the
candidate with the highest precision, marked fallback. All thresholds were written and hashed before any
evaluation label was read. Intervals are full-HARP bootstrap 95% intervals (1000 draws, seed 24) on the
evaluation year. A year meets the primary reading if the 0.50 alert tier delivers precision at least 0.50 and is
not a fallback year.

- The calibration promise transfers loosely. The 0.50 rule promised calibration recall 0.13–0.53 and delivered
  evaluation recall 0.10–0.72; precision landed at 0.72 and 0.81 in 2014 and 2017, at 0.54 in 2015, and below
  0.50 in 2016, 2022 and 2023.
- Where it works, the alert tier is sparse: 892, 1027 and 225 alarm windows in 2014, 2015 and 2017 against
  9641, 7602 and 1270 for the watch tier, with 63 of 83, 24 of 51 and 2 of 6 positive runs never alarmed by the
  alert tier. The watch tier still catches those.
- Solar cycle 25 is the limit. In 2022 the calibration year cannot reach precision 0.50 at all; in 2023 the rule
  alarms 185 windows and 30 of 38 positive runs get no alert-tier alarm. The oracle confirms that no threshold
  reaches 0.50 above recall 0.15 (2022) and 0.07 (2023).
- Intervals are wide in quiet years: 2016 and 2017 have 7 and 6 positive runs, so bootstrap draws without any
  positive region give lower bounds of 0.

## Limits

Already inspected years: adaptive development evidence, not an independent test. Counts are overlapping forecast
windows and groupings of them, not physical flares. The alert tier at precision 0.50 alarms on 185–1027 windows
per year and its precision rests on few regions; the interval must be read with the point. A two-tier
definition does not improve the model: the watch tier is the unchanged operating point and the alert tier is a
subset of its alarms.
